The Reading / Listening - Likweli Monkeys - Level 6

Despite centuries of exploration, scientists continue to find incredible new species of wildlife. The latest discovery is a previously unknown monkey with distinctive orange lips and a deep roaring whoop. The primate was found in a rainforest in the Democratic Republic of Congo. It is just the fifth new species of African monkey to be found in the past 75 years. Locals in the area call the monkey Likweli. Scientists have given it the scientific name Colobus congoensis. Anthropologist Kate Detwiler said: "The discovery of Colobus congoensis is both a scientific triumph and a sobering reminder that some of Earth's rarest creatures may vanish before the world even knows they exist."

Anthropologists say the Likweli is a different type of monkey. It has a glossy black coat, long fur, and a flowing tail. It also has stunning orange lips and facial markings. Researchers from Florida Atlantic University said the Likweli has "distinctive skull, dental, and skeletal characteristics". These distinguish it from other known colobus monkeys. Study co-author Professor Christopher Gilbert concurred. He said: "We analyzed museum collections, examining skulls, pelts, and skeletal anatomy alongside specimens from known African colobus monkeys. Those comparisons allowed us to demonstrate conclusively that Colobus congoensis represents a distinct species."

Vocabulary

    Paragraph 1

      1. exploration a. A person who studies people and human cultures.
      2. distinctive b. A great success or victory.
      3. whoop c. The act of going to new places to learn about them.
      4. anthropologist d. To disappear suddenly.
      5. triumph e. Easy to see as different from others.
      6. sobering f. To make a loud cry like a monkey; the loud cry a monkey makes.
      7. vanish g. Making you think seriously about something important.

    Paragraph 2

      8. glossy h. In a way that clearly proves something.
      9. facial i. Smooth and shiny.
      10. skeletal j. Related to the face.
      11. distinguish k. The study of the body and its parts.
      12. anatomy l. To see or show the difference between things.
      13. specimens m. Related to bones or the skeleton.
      14. conclusively n. Plants, animals, or objects collected for study.

    Homework

    1. VOCABULARY EXTENSION: Choose several of the words from the text. Use a dictionary or Google’s search field (or another search engine) to build up more associations / collocations of each word.
    2. INTERNET: Search the Internet and find out more about this news story. Share what you discover with your partner(s) in the next lesson.
    3. MONKEYS: Make a poster about monkeys. Show your work to your classmates in the next lesson. Did you all have similar things?
    4. NATURE: Write a magazine article about requiring all school children to spend time in forests. Include imaginary interviews with people who are for and against this.
    Read what you wrote to your classmates in the next lesson. Write down any new words and expressions you hear from your partner(s).
    5. WHAT HAPPENED NEXT? Write a newspaper article about the next stage in this news story. Read what you wrote to your classmates in the next lesson. Give each other feedback on your articles.
    6. LETTER: Write a letter to an expert on monkeys. Ask him/her three questions about them. Give him/her three of your thoughts on monkeys. Read your letter to your partner(s) in your next lesson. Your partner(s) will answer your questions.

    A Few Additional Activities for Students

    Ask your students what they have read, seen or heard about this news in their own language. Students are likely to / may have have encountered this news in their L1 and therefore bring a background knowledge to the classroom.

    Get students to role play different characters from this news story.

    Ask students to keep track of this news and revisit it to discuss in your next class.

    Ask students to male predictions of how this news might develop in the next few days or weeks, and then revisit and discuss in a future class.

    Ask students to write a follow-up story to this news.

    Students role play a journalist and someone who witnessed or was a part of this news. Perhaps they could make a video of the interview.

    Ask students to keep a news journal in English and add this story to their thoughts.
